Telescope · fluid dynamics

Filtered-air ventilation for a 700 mm telescope

A system that blows filtered air into the closed tube of a 700 mm Ritchey-Chrétien telescope. It keeps dust off the primary mirror without sealing the instrument, and brings the optics to air temperature quickly before observing.

SizingStokes settling, dome air currents, heat exchange at the aperture
FanChosen on the flow–pressure curve with filter and duct losses
VerificationCFD in FreeCAD + OpenFOAM, smoke tests
ControlFrom the observatory PC, works offline
CFD set up · smoke tests to come
  1. Sized flow rate and outlet velocity from particle settling (Stokes' law), air currents inside the dome and heat exchange at the aperture.
  2. Selected the fan on its flow–pressure curve, accounting for filter and duct losses.
  3. Set up the flow check as a CFD simulation in FreeCAD and OpenFOAM, to be confirmed with smoke tests.
  4. Designed 3D-printed fittings for a fully reversible installation, with the fan controlled from the observatory PC even without internet.

OpenFOAM simulations. They assume the air is pushed in through a small side hatch.

Dome · power electronics

Motorised dome shutters

Motors and the complete control system for the two shutters of the GAPC ODV observatory dome. It runs on solar power and opens by radio from the observatory PC.

24 Vwiper motors, sized from measured currents
2-layerPCB, 125 × 104 mm, 2 oz copper, ready for production
10 yearsof hourly satellite weather data for the power budget
  1. Sized the 24 V motors from the running currents I measured, with estimated inrush currents.
  2. Built the radio chain: observatory PC, Wi-Fi to a 433 MHz bridge, and a receiver with reversible polarity. One radio code moves both shutters and each stops at its own limit switch.
  3. Designed the relay board: four 16 A relays, diodes steering the open and close branches, normally closed safety limit switches, and a 2200 µF hold-up that keeps the control powered during motor inrush.
  4. Drew the schematic and a two-layer PCB in KiCad, with traces sized for the inrush currents.
  5. Sized the battery and solar panel with an hourly simulation over ten years of real satellite weather data.
